This run uses a synthetic, survivorship-aware market with planted alphas, so we can check whether the pipeline finds what is really there. Absolute Sharpe ratios are optimistic (Gaussian noise, no crashes, no capacity limits). The same code runs on a real point-in-time panel; see the last section.

Each signal is rank-transformed to normal scores, then regressed on sector dummies and estimated beta every month. The residual is what we test. "Planted" marks signals that carry true alpha in the simulator; a good pipeline should select those and reject the rest.

Findings

What the scorecard says

  • The hurdle works both ways. All placebos and the zero-alpha size and low-vol signals were rejected. Momentum, which is genuinely planted but weak, reached only t = %%MOM_T%% in-sample and was dropped (full-sample t = %%MOM_TF%%). Raising the bar from 2 to 3 buys fewer false discoveries at the price of missing weak true signals.
  • Neutralisation strips out sector bets. Raw value has an IC of %%VAL_RAW%%, falling to %%VAL_NEU%% once sector is removed. Part of the raw B/P spread is structural sector cheapness that earns nothing.
  • Reversal is real but expensive. It turns over %%REV_TO%% of the book each month, so its Sharpe drops from %%REV_SR%% to %%REV_SRN%% at %%COST%% bps. Its edge lives in month one only.

Decay sorts the signals into two families. Reversal and revisions are fast: all their predictive power sits in the next month, which explains their turnover. Value and quality are slow: their IC barely changes over a year, so they can be traded monthly or even quarterly with little loss.

Combination

The composite, in and out of sample

Signals with in-sample t > %%TSEL%% enter the blend. The IC-weighted version sets each weight to the trailing 36-month mean IC, using only ICs whose returns had already been realised at the formation date, floored at zero. The composite is neutralised again before forming portfolios. At the end of the sample, revisions carried %%W_REV%% of the weight and reversal %%W_REVR%%.

Returns rise steadily across deciles, so the spread does not depend on one tail. Out of sample the IC-weighted composite returns %%ICW_RET%% a year gross and %%ICW_RETN%% net, with a worst drawdown of %%ICW_DD%%. Equal weighting is close behind (OOS IC %%EQ_IC%%, net Sharpe %%EQ_SRN%%), which is the usual finding: estimated weights rarely beat simple averages by much. The strategy stays profitable up to about %%ICW_BE%% bps one-way; at 50 bps the Sharpe falls to %%SR50%%.

Robustness

How much two common data errors would distort the results

The same pipeline is rerun with two mistakes introduced on purpose. Look-ahead: fundamentals are used at their fiscal period end instead of their publication date. Survivorship: only the %%SURV%% stocks still listed at the end of the sample are kept, which is what you get by downloading today's index members' price history.

ErrorSignalIC, correctIC, with errorSharpe, correctSharpe, with error

Look-ahead is the dangerous one. Using ROE one month before it was announced lifts the quality IC from %%LQ_PIT%% to %%LQ_B%% and the Sharpe from %%LQ_SRP%% to %%LQ_SRB%%. A Sharpe that high on a slow fundamental signal should always prompt a timing audit. Value barely moves because book-to-price changes slowly.

Survivorship bias goes in both directions. The survivors-only universe returns %%EW_SV%% a year against %%EW_PIT%% for the full universe, because the %%DELIST%% delisted stocks and their losses disappear. Factor results can be understated too: quality's Sharpe falls from %%SQ_SRP%% to %%SQ_SRB%%, because low-quality stocks that went bust (the short leg's best trades) are no longer in the data. You cannot predict the sign of the error, so the only fix is the correct data.

Method

How the study is built

Universe
%%STOCKS%% stocks over the sample, including %%DELIST%% that delisted. A stock is investable at month t if it has 13 months of history, an estimated beta, and is above the 20th percentile of market cap. Delisting returns are kept in the forward return.
Timing
A signal at month-end t uses only data public by t and is scored against the return in month t+1. Book value is lagged 4 months and ROE 2 months; revisions and prices are real-time.
Signals
Momentum (return over months t-11 to t-1), 1-month reversal, book-to-price, ROE, 3-month net EPS revisions, negative 12-month volatility, negative log market cap, and two random placebos.
Neutralisation
Rank to normal scores, regress on sector dummies plus 36-month rolling beta, take residuals, re-standardise.
Testing
Monthly Spearman IC with Newey-West t; equal-weighted decile long-short; IC decay to 12 months; turnover as half the sum of absolute weight changes over the gross book.
Selection
First 60% of months in-sample. A signal enters the composite only if its in-sample t exceeds %%TSEL%%, a stricter bar than the usual 2 that reflects how many signals get tried.
Costs
%%COST%% bps one-way on traded notional as the base case, with a grid from 0 to 50 bps. Weight drift within the month and market impact are ignored.
Limitations and next steps

What this does not show yet

  • Real markets have fat tails and factor crashes (momentum in 2009, value in 2018–2020). Expect real decile Sharpes well below these.
  • Decile portfolios ignore risk. The next step is a risk-model-based optimiser with turnover penalties and position limits.
  • Costs are a flat rate. A square-root impact model scaled by ADV would make capacity visible.
  • Fast signals could be traded less often or blended with slow ones before rebalancing, which would cut turnover more than it cuts IC.
  • The first real-data run should be US large and mid caps from a survivorship-free point-in-time source.
